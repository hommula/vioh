Bonus evidence: camera timing, rolling shutter, and onboard recovery

GNSS-free position hold on the Orqa M2-AI. Each number says where it comes from: our aircraft, recording of this aircraft type by the organizers, or simulation.

1. Timing and rolling shutter

The problem. The camera reads a picture row by row, and its timestamp is not the moment the picture was taken. The aircraft tilts in between. One degree of tilt moves the picture by about 6 pixels, which at 50 m height looks like 0.9 m of movement.

Our treatment (src/hold.hpp, levelPoints): each tracked point becomes a viewing ray through the fisheye lens model; the time of that point's own image row is computed (frame timestamp + camera delay - readout time x share of the picture below the row); the gyro attitude at exactly that time (1000 samples a second, same clock) rotates the ray to what a level camera would see. Only then are pictures compared.

2. Onboard recovery

The rule. The judge covers the camera for 3 s in the middle of the loop. Back at the start mark the displayed position must be within 0.5 m of zero.

What happens while covered: NOT READY in under 0.1 s (measured), roll, pitch and yaw 1500, position frozen. After uncovering: tracking starts again from the frozen position. In addition, four times a second the picture is compared directly with a "home picture" stored at the zero (400 corners with descriptors, corrected for lens, tilt and row time). When they match, the position is taken from that match alone.
